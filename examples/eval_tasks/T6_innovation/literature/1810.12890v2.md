# DropBlock: A regularization method for convolutional networks

- arXiv: 1810.12890
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12890

## Abstract

Deep neural networks often work well when they have enough capacity, but may
overfit when the training set is small or the model is large. Dropout has been a
standard regularizer, but it is less effective for convolutional layers because
adjacent pixels share spatial information: dropping individual pixels still
leaves correlated information. We propose DropBlock, a structured form of
dropout that drops CONTIGUOUS regions (blocks) of a feature map. DropBlock
outperforms dropout on CIFAR-10: an AmoebaNet with DropBlock reaches 98.5% test
accuracy (state of the art at publication), with consistent gains across
ResNet/WideResNet/AmoebaNet.

## Key Method

- During training, zero out a block (e.g. 7x7) of each feature map channel at a
  sampled location; keep feature maps unchanged at inference.
- Two knobs: block_size and keep_prob (per-channel dropout probability), with a
  linear schedule of keep_prob from 1.0 to target over training.
- Works alongside dropout on fully-connected layers; complements data-side
  regularization (mixup/CutMix).

## Why it matters for this project

The conv-layer counterpart of dropout: if the model is overfitting (train
accuracy high, test gap large), DropBlock on conv features is a stronger
regularizer than the plain dropout already tried — and it is citable as its
own method [arXiv:1810.12890].
